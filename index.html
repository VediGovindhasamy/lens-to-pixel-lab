Sensor output · 24 × 18 pixels

Raw sensor view, seen from behind the camera: the image lands upside down and mirrored, like the camera obscura. Brightness is display-boosted so blurred light stays visible; darker still means less light.

Try this

  1. Point → pixel. With “one point of each object”, the warm fan (near F) and the cool fan (far T) each fill the lens, then each meets at its own point.
  2. Focus = moving the lens. Press “Focus on near” and watch the lens slide away from the fixed sensor; “Focus on far” slides it back toward the sensor. Near objects need the lens farther out.
  3. Depth of field. Focus on near: the green zone sits tightly around the F, the T is outside it and blurred. Close the aperture f/2 → f/16: the green zone stretches back until it reaches the T, and the T turns sharp. The image also gets darker.
  4. Open it again. At f/1.4 the T blurs even more: shallow depth of field. Switch to “Focus on far” and the roles swap.
  5. Only the lens-to-sensor side changes. The light arriving at the lens is identical at every F-number. The amber arrow across the iris shows the opening; rays hitting the blades stop there, so fewer rays continue to the sensor.
